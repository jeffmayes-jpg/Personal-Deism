Truth Is to Be Pursued Wherever It Leads


Scientific discovery, philosophical reasoning, and sincere spiritual exploration are all valuable avenues toward greater understanding.


Science, philosophy, and spiritual exploration are complementary personal means of approaching truth; they are not themselves the transcendent reality.


Truth concerns whether a proposition corresponds to reality. Justification concerns whether a person has adequate reason to believe that proposition.


A test, once established, must be applied universally. It does not change according to the entity being tested, the evidence available to that entity, or the knowledge possessed by it. Differences in available evidence or knowledge may affect what can be established in an individual examination, but they do not alter the test itself or the standards by which its results are evaluated.

The test must precede the conclusion. It may be revised through thoughtful consideration when examination demonstrates that revision is warranted, but any revised test must likewise be applicable universally. No entity may be exempted from the test, nor may a test be selectively constructed or applied to produce a desired conclusion.


Personal Deism is not defined by possessing truths that belong to it; it is defined, in part, by its commitment to recognizing and applying truths wherever they are found.


Question

Does Personal Deism claim ownership of the principles it embraces?


Response

No. Personal Deism does not claim that the truths or principles it embraces belong exclusively to it. A sound principle does not become the property of a philosophy merely because that philosophy recognizes and adopts it. It may therefore adopt and embody a principle without claiming ownership of it.


Objection

Truth may undermine cherished beliefs.


Response

If beliefs cannot withstand honest investigation, they should be refined. Truth takes precedence over comfort. New evidence points to, perhaps, new conclusions. There is always more to learn.
