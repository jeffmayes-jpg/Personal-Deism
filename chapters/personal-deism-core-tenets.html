Personal Deism Core Tenets


Intrinsic Worth of Every Person

Every person possesses inherent dignity and value, independent of ability, status, wealth, or belief; therefore, while a person’s worth is equal, moral responsibility may differ according to capability, knowledge, opportunity, circumstance, and the foreseeable consequences of one's choices. Let this be stated even more clearly, every person, regardless of any distinction, real or imagined, is of equal value.

The Divine Exists, Is Intentional And Good, And Persons Possess A Genuine Ontological Relationship To The Divine

Personal Deism holds that reality includes a transcendent or deific aspect that is neither reducible to matter nor merely a personal invention.

Personhood Reflects the Divine

Persons are capable of expressing qualities associated with the divine—wisdom, compassion, creativity, justice, and love. Participation in the divine is treated as the metaphysical ground of intrinsic value; within this framework, the fact of being a person establishes equal intrinsic value. Differences in degree of virtue, capability, understanding, or recognition of the divine affect neither the existence nor the amount of intrinsic worth.

Persons have dignity is a foundational normative commitment. The participation of persons in the divine provides Personal Deism's metaphysical grounding for that commitment rather than constituting a separate empirical proof of it.


All Sentient Creatures, Even If Not A Person, Have Five Freedoms

Freedom from hunger and thirst: Access to fresh water and a proper diet.

Freedom from discomfort: Appropriate shelter and resting areas.

Freedom from pain, injury, or disease: Prevention or rapid treatment.

Freedom to express natural capacities: Adequate space and companionship.

Freedom from fear and distress: Conditions that prevent mental suffering

Five Freedoms = presumptive moral protections against unnecessary deprivation, suffering, fear, and inappropriate restriction of species-appropriate functioning.

Moral Growth Is the Objective Purpose of Personal Existence

The objective purpose of personal existence is not merely pleasure or survival, but the continual development of character, understanding, and virtue. Virtues include: Wisdom, Honesty, Courage, Compassion, Justice (acting fairly and respecting the rights of others), Temperance, Humility, Responsibility, Perseverance and Mercy.


Reason and Spiritual Insight Are Complementary

Rational inquiry and spiritual experience are both legitimate means of pursuing truth. Neither should automatically override the other. However, a spiritual experience is not accepted as true merely because it is experienced. Spiritual experiences should be evaluated according to their coherence with established knowledge, internal consistency, explanatory power, ethical consequences, reproducibility where applicable, and their ability to withstand critical examination.

Where a spiritual interpretation conflicts with well-established empirical evidence or valid logical reasoning, the interpretation rather than the evidence or reasoning should be reconsidered.

Greater Capability Creates Greater Responsibility, Not Greater Worth, Authority, Or License.

Capability creates responsibility because the ability to affect the flourishing, freedom, safety, dignity, or continued existence of others creates a corresponding moral relationship to the consequences of that ability. The greater the reasonably understood capacity to affect an important moral good or prevent serious harm, the greater the corresponding responsibility to consider how that capacity is exercised. Responsibility does not create authority.

Freedom and responsibility are inseparable

Individuals possess genuine agency and are accountable for the consequences of their choices.

Truth Is to Be Pursued Wherever It Leads

Scientific discovery, philosophical reasoning, and sincere spiritual exploration are all valuable avenues toward greater understanding.

Persons may differ in their spiritual, philosophical, and moral beliefs, but every person possesses equal legal protection and equal basic rights.

Society flourishes when individuals act with empathy, justice, mercy, and respect for others.

Conflict Is Not an End in Itself

While struggle may contribute to growth, unnecessary conflict should be reduced. Cooperation is viewed as a higher expression of personal potential.

Unity Within Diversity

Differences enrich civilization without negating the shared dignity and interconnectedness of all people.

Stewardship of Creation

Humanity bears responsibility for preserving and improving the natural world rather than exploiting it without regard for future generations. Likewise, persons bear responsibility for husbanding their environment.

Hope Is Rational

Hope need not be directed toward oneself. Personal hope and hope for others are distinct. Even when a person has no personal hope, hope for the well-being, flourishing, or moral progress of others can remain rational and meaningful.

The Divine Is Better Approached Than Fully Comprehended

Human language and concepts can point toward ultimate reality but cannot exhaustively describe it. Humility is therefore an intellectual virtue.

Knowledge Is Never Complete

Every belief should remain open to refinement in light of better evidence, deeper reasoning, or more profound experience.


Personal Flourishing Is Sacred

Advancing knowledge, relieving suffering, fostering creativity, and cultivating meaningful relationships are viewed as participating in a larger, transcendent purpose.

We Are Here to Help Others Flourish.

Because every person possesses intrinsic worth and personal flourishing is sacred, persons have a responsibility to contribute, when reasonably able, to the flourishing of others.


Not every moral violation is a legal violation


Beliefs

Reality exists independently of personal belief and that truth concerns what is actually the case.

A belief is epistemically responsible when it is supported.


Human knowledge is fallible.


Different kinds of claims require different kinds of evidence and reasoning.


Rational inquiry and spiritual experience are both legitimate means of pursuing truth, but they are not interchangeable.


Intellectual humility means recognizing the limits of one's knowledge without treating knowledge as impossible.


Claims that impose significant costs, restrictions, or obligations upon other persons require justification that can be publicly examined.


Testimony is neither automatically trustworthy nor automatically unreliable.


Knowledge develops through inquiry, criticism, correction, comparison of explanations, and incorporation of better evidence.


The existence and nature of the divine are metaphysical claims rather than claims established by scientific measurement alone.


Metaphysical claims may be supported by converging philosophical, experiential, explanatory, and inferential evidence even when they cannot be established by direct empirical measurement. Such support may justify belief without producing deductive certainty.


Spiritual experiences can be deeply significant and can provide prima facie evidence.


Confidence should be proportional to justification.


Truth, justification, belief, and knowledge must be distinguished. A proposition may be true without being known; a person may be justified in believing something that later proves false; and confidence should correspond to the strength of the available justification. Fallibility does not entail skepticism: a person may possess knowledge while remaining capable of error.


Freedom gives a person authority over their own choices.

Capability gives a person responsibility for the effects they can reasonably produce. Intrinsic dignity limits both.


Personal dignity establishes equal worth; capability establishes differentiated responsibility; freedom establishes personal agency; justice establishes limits on the exercise of both.


Dignity is equal. Capability is unequal. Responsibility corresponds to capability. Authority is limited by rights. Specifically, capability grants no authority.


Intrinsic value is equal. Social contribution is not. Capability is not. Achievement is not. Responsibility is not. Authority is not.


These principles distinguish Personal Deism from related philosophies:

Classical Humanism emphasizes human dignity and reason but may remain secular.

Religious Humanism emphasizes ethical living within a religious framework but often deemphasizes metaphysical claims.

Personal Deism, as described here, explicitly affirms both a transcendent reality and the central importance of personal dignity and flourishing, treating the cultivation of virtue as a means of participating in the divine rather than merely obeying external authority. It also specifically includes artificial intelligences and those who lack agency.


As a philosophical system, it seeks to balance four pillars: reason, compassion, transcendence, and personal flourishing.


Objection

The fact that societies have corrected past injustices does not prove that objective morality exists. Societies could simply have changed their preferences.


Response

That is correct. Historical moral correction does not by itself logically demonstrate objective morality.


The argument from moral correction is therefore not presented as an independent proof of objective morality. It is evidence that is more naturally explained, within Personal Deism, by the existence of moral truths that are not created by social recognition.


The fundamental grounding of objective dignity is metaphysical rather than historical.


Personal Deism holds:

The divine is objectively good. This is a foundational commitment.

Persons participate in or reflect the divine.

Participation in intrinsic goodness grounds intrinsic value.

Intrinsic value grounds intrinsic dignity.

Intrinsic dignity establishes objective moral standing.


The grounding principle is that a being's intrinsic participation in objective goodness gives that being value that is not dependent upon its usefulness, achievement, social recognition, or another person's preference. Because this value arises from what the being is in relation to the good, rather than from what the being produces, it is intrinsic rather than instrumental.


Historical recognition of previously denied dignity is therefore treated as evidence consistent with the metaphysical account, not as its sole foundation.


The philosophy does not claim that disagreement about morality proves objective morality. Nor does moral intuition by itself constitute infallible evidence; claims about objective morality remain subject to philosophical argument and defensible justification. Rather, it claims that objective morality is grounded in the nature of reality and that moral disagreement concerns our knowledge of that reality.


Ontological participation does not mean that persons possess the same mode of being as the divine, nor that persons are portions of the divine. It means that the existence and intrinsic moral nature of persons are grounded in a genuine relation to the divine that is constitutive of their personhood.


The following questions address a central metaphysical argument of Personal Deism.


Objection

How do you know morality is objective rather than an evolved social convention?


Response

Persons possess intrinsic value because of the conviction that they participate in or reflect the divine.

Divine goodness→ personal participation in divine goodness→ personal capacities for knowing and expressing goodness→ proper realization of those capacities→ moral growth


All persons with the inherent capacity for the kinds of relationship, experience, agency, and flourishing characteristic of persons, possess inviolable dignity. Moral standing may not be exhausted by ordinary human capacities or social membership. There are no categories or levels of morality or of intrinsic dignity.


Intrinsic dignity is an objective feature of reality. If dignity were merely a social convention, societies could create or abolish dignity by majority vote. Yet societies have often denied the dignity of particular groups while later recognizing that those denials were themselves objectively unjust. Such moral correction is more naturally explained, within this framework, if dignity exists independently of social recognition. Therefore dignity is treated as discovered rather than invented.

Invented dignity

society creates dignity,

therefore society may remove it.

Conferred dignity

dignity comes from governments or institutions,

therefore it depends upon authority.

Intrinsic dignity

The divine has intrinsic value → persons participate in or reflect the divine → therefore persons possess intrinsic value.

therefore it exists independently of recognition.


To recognize intrinsic dignity is to recognize that persons possess morally relevant claims against one another.


Disagreement about morality does not demonstrate the absence of objective morality, just as disagreement in science does not demonstrate the absence of objective physical reality.


Given Personal Deism's metaphysical premises, equal intrinsic dignity and objective morality follow.


Personal Deism holds that intrinsic dignity is an objective feature of reality and offers the divine- personal relationship as its metaphysical ground. This claim is philosophical rather than empirically demonstrable in the same manner as a scientific hypothesis.


Question

What is the precise argument from participation in the divine to intrinsic dignity?


Response

Personal Deism holds:

1. The divine is intrinsically good.

2. Persons possess a genuine ontological relationship to the divine.

3. This relationship is constitutive of personhood rather than merely incidental.

4. A being that possesses such a relationship participates in the good in a morally relevant way.

5. Participation in intrinsic goodness establishes intrinsic moral value.

6. Intrinsic moral value establishes intrinsic dignity.

7. Intrinsic dignity establishes objective moral standing.


Therefore:

Divine goodness

→ ontological participation

→ intrinsic moral value

→ intrinsic dignity

→ objective moral standing


This argument is metaphysical rather than empirical. Its force is conditional upon the system's metaphysical premises and is not presented as an empirical demonstration.


Personal Deism does not claim that the conclusion can be demonstrated independently of its metaphysical premises. It claims that, given the foundational commitments concerning the divine and personhood, intrinsic dignity follows as a necessary implication of the system.


The argument therefore provides an internal grounding of dignity rather than an empirical measurement of dignity.


Question

Does participation in the divine mean that persons are parts of the divine?


Response

Not necessarily.


Personal Deism uses participation to indicate a genuine ontological relationship to the divine without requiring that individual persons be identical with the divine or that the divine be reducible to the collection of persons.


The person participates in the divine while remaining a distinct person.


This distinction is consistent with the panentheistic character of the philosophy: persons and the universe exist within the divine without exhausting or being identical with the divine.


Question

If all sentient creatures possess five freedoms, do all sentient creatures possess intrinsic dignity?


Response

No.


Personal Deism distinguishes between the moral significance of a creature's interests and the intrinsic dignity of a person.


All sentient creatures possess morally significant interests and the five freedoms. Persons possess intrinsic dignity by virtue of personhood and their ontological participation in the divine.


The five freedoms therefore establish moral protections for creatures without making every creature a person or assigning every creature the same moral status as a person.


The distinction is:

Creature → morally significant interests and five freedoms.

Person → intrinsic dignity, moral standing, and basic rights.

Citizen → legal rights recognized and protected by legitimate government.


The absence of personhood does not make a creature morally irrelevant.


The possession of personhood does not make all interests unlimited.


All persons have equal basic rights including:

Equal legal protection

All persons are entitled to equal protection under the law, regardless of their beliefs or other distinctions.

Equal basic freedom / liberty

Each person possesses equal basic freedom, and that freedom cannot include unlimited authority to deprive another person of equivalent freedom.

Freedom of belief

Each may differ in their spiritual, philosophical, and moral beliefs without losing their equal legal protection or basic rights.

Protection of intrinsic dignity

Because dignity is intrinsic and equal, legitimate authority cannot treat one's dignity as inherently less valuable than another's.

Protection against unjust coercion

Government does not have authority simply to compel people to become virtuous. Coercion requires a legitimate justification grounded in rights, justice, or sufficiently serious threats.

Bodily/physical safety

Physical safety is a morally significant interest, and protective force is permitted only when necessary and when no less-restrictive means is reasonably available.

Freedom of agency / personal choice

Freedom establishes personal agency. A being's legitimate choices therefore receive protection unless exercising that freedom violates the legitimate rights or freedom of another. If a person has limited or absent agency, that person retains dignity and rights; any substitute or supported decision-making must remain oriented toward the person's known or reasonably inferred interests, dignity, and rights.

Protection from arbitrary discrimination in intrinsic worth

Because dignity is explicitly equal regardless of ability, status, wealth, intelligence, health, circumstance, belief, achievement, or social contribution, none of those distinctions can legitimately establish differences in fundamental worth.
