Personhood Reflects the Divine


Persons are capable of expressing qualities associated with the divine—wisdom, compassion, creativity, justice, and love, to name some.


As an individual grows, they become more expressive of the virtues: Wisdom, Honesty, Courage, Compassion, Justice (acting fairly and respecting the rights of others), Temperance, Humility, Responsibility, Perseverance and Mercy.


Because participation is constitutive of the person's relation to the good rather than merely an external association, the person's relation to goodness is intrinsic to what the person is; intrinsic moral value therefore follows from the person's nature rather than from achievement, recognition, or contingent circumstance.


Question

Why does reflecting the divine produce equal worth?


Response

Every person has a kind of inviolable standing because every person possesses or is capable of a distinct form of relation to the divine, regardless of the degree to which that capacity is exercised.


Objection

Persons are capable of cruelty as well as compassion.


Response

The tenet states that persons are capable of expressing divine qualities, not that they always do. Moral growth consists of increasingly expressing those virtues.


Question

How does reflection of the divine provide grounding of personal dignity?


Response

The grounding of personal dignity is not because of the metaphysical properties of the divine, but of the orientation towards what is good.
