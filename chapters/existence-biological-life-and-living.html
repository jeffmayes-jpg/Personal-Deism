Existence, Biological Life, and Living


Personal Deism distinguishes between existence, biological life, and living.


Existence is the most fundamental category. To exist is to have being. For a living person, existence as a person does not depend upon ability, social participation, productivity, independence, or fulfillment of a social role.


Biological life is a particular mode of personal existence possessed by living organisms. A biologically living human being is alive by virtue of the continued functioning of the organism. Biological life does not depend upon social participation, productivity, independence, or fulfillment of a social role.


Living, in the fuller existential sense, refers to the active expression and experience of life. It may include participation, relationship, activity, purpose, experience, growth, contribution, and engagement with the world. Consequently, a person may be biologically alive without considering themselves to be fully living in this existential sense.


Thus: Existence is not identical to biological life, and biological life is not identical to living in the fuller existential sense.


A person may therefore exist as a person and be biologically alive while not considering themselves to be living in the fuller existential or social sense.


These distinctions do not establish that social participation is necessary for biological life, nor do they establish that social participation is necessary for personhood. A person who lacks social interaction remains a person.


Personal Deism makes no claim here concerning the existence or status of a person after biological death. Questions concerning deceased persons are outside this particular distinction.


Accordingly:

Existence → having being

Biological life → being a living organism

Living → actively participating in and experiencing life in the fuller existential sense

Personhood → the fundamental status of the person


These categories may overlap, but they are not synonymous.
