Trust is given. Distrust is earned.


One’s default position is one of trust, not distrust.
Many communities espouse such, few achieve it. For example: innocent until proven guilty in a court of law. Such is a noble sentiment, seldom achieved. Impartiality is rare. Public opinion may affect perseption or reaction. Personal feelings may affect perception or reaction.


Question

Does mean trust everyone and everything?


Response

No. It means that one’s position begins with trust, rather than distrust. For example, hearsay is to be believed unless there is reason to doubt it. Avoid the presumption that someone is lying. That is different than accepting their statement as truth.


Question

Does that mean abandon caution?


Response

No. Prudence may suggest caution. Caution may be reasonable. However, such caution must not be due to distrust, unless warranted.


Question

Is trusting therefore an absolute obligation to trust?


Response

No. Trust is the default, and may be modified by experience.


Question

Is trust a gift we owe every person by virtue of their personhood, or is it an initial disposition we choose to extend to every person?


Response

Both. Trust is an initial disposition which you must choose to extend, and is owed to all, but the grounds for withdrawing it are personal.
