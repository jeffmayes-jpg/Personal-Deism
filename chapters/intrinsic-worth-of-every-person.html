Intrinsic Worth of Every Person


Every person possesses inherent dignity and value, independent of capabilities, achievements, social contribution, recognition of the divine, or social status.


Whatever means by which the individual is distinguished is insignificant compared to the undeniable fact that they are a person, having inherent value and dignity.


If the divine is good (per the foundational commitment), if persons possess an ontological participation in the divine, and if participation in intrinsic goodness grounds intrinsic moral value, then objective personal dignity follows.


Question

Why is the divine good?


Response

Goodness is not something the divine possesses as an accidental attribute; goodness belongs to the nature of the divine itself and is a foundational commitment.


The necessity of divine goodness follows from Personal Deism's conception of the divine as the ultimate ground of objective goodness rather than as a being that merely happens to possess goodness. If the divine could be fundamentally evil, goodness would not be grounded in the divine and the claim that moral reality is an expression of the divine would fail. Personal Deism therefore treats necessary divine goodness as a foundational metaphysical commitment rather than as a conclusion derived from contingent observations.

This necessity is conceptual rather than evidentiary: if the divine is the ultimate ontological ground of objective goodness, then goodness cannot be external to or contrary to the nature of the divine. The claim that the divine is the ultimate ground of objective goodness is itself a metaphysical premise requiring independent philosophical justification.


This does not mean that every event occurring within the universe is good, nor that every action attributed to the divine is thereby justified. Human interpretations of divine action remain fallible and subject to rational and ethical examination.


Question

Why does the divine have value?


Response

Morality is neither arbitrary divine command nor external law imposed on the divine; rather, moral reality is grounded in the goodness of the divine, which is a foundational commitment.


The statement that the divine is good is not empirical, but metaphysical, and a foundational commitment.


Objection

Personal worth is not intrinsic; it is earned through contribution.


Response

Contribution may affect a person's achievements or reputation, but not their inherent dignity. If dignity depended upon ability, the disabled, elderly, or infants would possess less worth, contradicting the equal personhood from which dignity arises.


Question
If personhood depends on consciousness, self-awareness, rational capacity, and the capacity to participate in morally significant relationships, but those characteristics need not all be simultaneously exercised, what principled method determines when an entity has enough of the relevant underlying capacities to qualify as a person, particularly in borderline cases?


Response

Personhood is determined holistically from the totality of evidence concerning an entity's underlying capacities and nature, rather than by requiring a particular number of criteria to be demonstrated. Suppose scientists discover an unfamiliar animal species. They determine that members of the species: are clearly conscious; appear to recognize themselves; communicate extensively; solve fairly sophisticated problems; form long-term social relationships; and appear to understand other individuals' intentions. Their rational capacity necessary for personhood remains undetermined. Do they qualify as persons? Yes. Consciousness and self-awareness are the most fundamental indicators, while rational capacity and morally significant relational capacity provide additional evidence of personhood. No single criterion functions as an absolute threshold.


Holistic evaluation is not merely considering an aggregate number, nor does the existence of one or more capacities or natures determine the outcome of the evaluation. The evaluation is not predetermined by any factor.


Question

Why should all persons possess equal dignity?


Response

All persons possess equal dignity because all participate in the divine, while the particular application and scope of rights may vary according to relevant interests, vulnerability, capacity, and circumstance, equal basic rights remain universal.


Question

How do you know persons have intrinsic value?


Response

Persons possess a distinct ontological relation to the divine that grounds their intrinsic dignity.

Participation in the divine is not merely causal association with the divine; it constitutes a person's ontological relationship to the divine and is therefore sufficient to ground intrinsic worth.

Ontological means that it explores what it means for something to actually exist.


Question

Does equal intrinsic worth mean that every person has equal practical value in every circumstance?


Response

No.


Personal Deism distinguishes intrinsic worth from practical value, social contribution, capability, achievement, and usefulness.


Every person possesses equal intrinsic worth.

Persons may nevertheless differ greatly in:

  • Capability

  • Resources

  • Achievement

  • Knowledge

  • Skill

  • Responsibility

  • Social contribution

  • Influence

  • Relationships

  • Practical usefulness in particular circumstances


These differences do not establish differences in intrinsic dignity.


A person may therefore have greater capability or make a greater contribution without possessing greater intrinsic worth.


Likewise, a person may make little measurable contribution while retaining full intrinsic dignity.

The distinction is fundamental:

Worth concerns what a person is.

Capability concerns what a person can do.

Achievement concerns what a person has accomplished.

Contribution concerns what a person contributes.

Responsibility concerns what a person ought reasonably to do.

Authority concerns what a person may legitimately require of others


These categories must not be conflated.


Question

Why does intrinsic dignity necessarily entail moral obligations?


Response

To recognize a person's intrinsic dignity is to recognize that the person possesses a legitimate claim against treatment inconsistent with that dignity.


Question

Does equal dignity require identical treatment?


Response

No.


Equal dignity means that every person possesses equal intrinsic worth and equal basic moral standing.

It does not mean that every person has identical needs, capabilities, circumstances, responsibilities, or legitimate interests.


Justice may therefore require different treatment when relevant differences affect the legitimate purpose of the treatment.

Such differences must not be used to establish differences in intrinsic worth.


The distinction is:

Equal dignity → equal intrinsic worth.

Relevant differences → potentially different treatment.

Irrelevant distinctions → cannot legitimately establish differences in fundamental worth.

Therefore, equality does not require treating unlike circumstances as though they were identical. It requires that differences in treatment be justified by morally relevant considerations rather than by arbitrary judgments of worth.


Question

Why does intrinsic value generate an obligation?


Response

Whatever possesses intrinsic moral value thereby constitutes a morally relevant reason for action that is not dependent upon another person's recognition, preference, or benefit.


Question

Why does being a person entail intrinsic dignity?


Response

The divine has intrinsic value → persons participate in or reflect the divine → therefore persons possess intrinsic value.
