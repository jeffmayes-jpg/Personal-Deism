Personal Flourishing Is Sacred


Advancing knowledge, relieving suffering, fostering creativity, and cultivating meaningful relationships are viewed as participating in a larger, transcendent purpose.


It is an individual’s sacred duty to personally flourish and to aid others to thrive when reasonably able and when doing so is consistent with the equal dignity, rights, and legitimate flourishing of all affected persons. Note that the government has no authority to compel this.


Question

What does it mean to call personal flourishing a sacred duty?


Response

A sacred duty is a moral responsibility grounded in the transcendent significance attributed to human flourishing.


It does not mean that government possesses unlimited authority to compel the duty.


It does not mean that a person must sacrifice their own legitimate flourishing whenever another person could benefit.


Because every person possesses equal intrinsic dignity, one's own flourishing is itself morally significant.


The duty to aid others must therefore be understood together with the duty to respect one's own dignity, legitimate freedom, responsibilities, and basic flourishing.


A person should contribute to the flourishing of others when reasonably able, while maintaining appropriate boundaries and recognizing that not every opportunity to benefit another person creates an enforceable obligation.


Sacred therefore describes the moral and transcendent significance of flourishing, not an unlimited governmental power to compel it.


Question

Does this require sacrificing oneself entirely for others?


Response

The document states that aiding others to thrive is a sacred duty, but it does not say that one's own flourishing is excluded. Since every person possesses intrinsic worth, the individual's flourishing is also part of the moral good.


Question

What is flourishing?


Response

Basic Flourishing

Conditions necessary for a person to live as a functioning human being.

Personal Flourishing

The individual's own development according to their legitimate values and capacities.

Relational Flourishing

Healthy relationships and communities.

Moral Flourishing

Development of virtue and character.

Transcendent Flourishing

Participation in or orientation toward the divine.


Each form is distinct and non-reducible, while remaining capable of supporting and influencing the others.


Question

If the forms of flourishing are independent, can one form exist without the others?


Response

Independence means that no form of flourishing is reducible to another. It does not mean that the forms are unrelated.


Basic flourishing can provide conditions for personal flourishing.

Personal flourishing can strengthen relational flourishing.

Relational flourishing can support moral flourishing.

Moral flourishing can deepen transcendent orientation.

Transcendent orientation can in turn influence personal, relational, and moral flourishing.


No form is identical to another, but they may mutually support one another.


Personal Deism therefore distinguishes between:

Non-reducibility — one form of flourishing is not merely another form under a different name.

Interdependence — different forms of flourishing may support or affect one another.


Question

Can an atheist achieve personal flourishing?


Response

A person may achieve basic, personal, relational, and moral flourishing without recognizing the divine, but Personal Deism holds that transcendent flourishing requires orientation toward the divine.


Participation in personal flourishing does not require religious or spiritual acknowledgment of the divine.
