Personal Deism


This document is a foundational philosophical commitment. Its foundational commitments are held as starting points for inquiry, not as claims of infallibility, and all non-foundational conclusions remain open to revision in light of sound reasoning and relevant evidence. Note that foundational commitments may subsequently be supported by internal argument.


Personal Deism is a coherent metaphysical-ethical philosophical system with a developed theory of personhood, intrinsic dignity, moral responsibility, flourishing, epistemology, and political morality.


A foundational commitment may serve as a starting point for reasoning within Personal Deism while remaining vulnerable to rational challenge. If compelling reasons arise to reject or substantially revise a foundational commitment, the system must either revise that commitment and its dependent conclusions or acknowledge that those conclusions no longer follow. Foundational status therefore establishes logical priority within the system, not immunity from criticism.


Metaphysical claims

Metaphysical claims concern the fundamental nature, structure, or grounding of reality. They cannot always be tested by direct empirical observation. They should nevertheless be evaluated through conceptual clarity, logical coherence, explanatory power, compatibility with established knowledge, comparison with competing metaphysical explanations, and the absence of decisive defeaters.

A metaphysical claim may therefore be rationally held without being empirically demonstrable, but its status should not be confused with that of an empirically established claim.

Personal Deism consequently distinguishes between foundational commitment, metaphysical inference, empirical knowledge, and practical conclusion. A conclusion may be rationally supported without possessing the same epistemic status as an empirical observation.


Community dialogue and education is encouraged. Ongoing philosophical and theological reflection is encouraged, including open forums for debate, and mechanisms for revising core beliefs based on new insights or evidence. Open disagreement can expose hidden assumptions, reveal errors, and improve understanding.


Testimony is a legitimate source of knowledge, but its reliability depends upon factors such as the competence, honesty, access to information, consistency, corroboration, and circumstances of the source.


An important thing to note is that Personal Deism is more than simply Aristotelian virtue ethics with the divine added.


No entity is exempt from the test for personhood, and no entity is excluded from it by classification.


What someone—or Personal Deism—espouses is a suggestion, not an obligation.


Question

What qualifies a being as a person?


Response

A person is a being whose nature includes the capacities constitutive of personhood: consciousness, self-awareness, rational capacity, and the capacity to participate in morally significant relationships. These criteria are understood as characteristics of the being's nature, not as a demand for uninterrupted exercise of every capacity. All of these characteristics are not required to be simultaneously present for personhood. As an obvious example, one does not lose personhood because of a loss of consciousness.


These characteristics establish a personal subject of experience, relationship, and intrinsic moral standing.


A person need not presently exercise every characteristic associated with personhood. Personhood is grounded in the nature and capacities of the being rather than in continuous performance of those capacities.


The characteristics are constitutive dimensions of personhood considered collectively, and that temporary absence of one or more capacities does not negate the underlying personal nature of the being.


Human personhood is not protected merely because humans belong to Homo sapiens. Nonhuman origin does not automatically exclude personhood.


Every person is sentient, but not every sentient creature is a person.


The distinction between personhood and sentience does not imply that sentient nonpersons lack moral significance. Sentience establishes morally relevant interests and therefore grounds the five fundamental freedoms, while personhood establishes the additional moral standing associated with intrinsic dignity and basic rights.


Question

What does deific mean?


Response

Deific refers to the divine. It does not refer to any specific deity or concept thereof. The term therefore identifies the philosophy's orientation toward the divine without prescribing a single theology or named deity.


Question

How does Personal Deism identify an underlying personal nature in cases of permanent severe impairment, early development, altered consciousness, or radically unfamiliar artificial minds.


Response

A lack of agency, in and of itself, is insufficient to make a declaration of non-personhood.


A biological being whose nature includes the capacities constitutive of personhood is a person, even when those capacities are temporarily or not undeveloped, impaired, or not presently exercised.


Question

Can an artificial intelligence be a person?


Response

Potentially, yes.


Personal Deism does not define personhood exclusively by biological origin, species membership, embodiment, or human ancestry.


A being qualifies as a person when it possesses the intrinsic characteristics that establish a personal subject of experience, including consciousness, self-awareness, rational capacity, and the capacity for moral significance or moral relationship.


Therefore, an artificial intelligence would qualify as a person if it genuinely possessed the relevant characteristics of personhood.


The mere production of random, incoherent, or purely imitative language is not sufficient to establish personhood. However, meaningful linguistic communication, particularly when accompanied by self-awareness, rational reflection, and recognition of self and other, constitutes significant evidence of the underlying capacities relevant to personhood.


The relevant question is not whether a being is biologically human, but whether it genuinely possesses the characteristics that make personal experience, relationship, and intrinsic moral standing possible.


Temporary, developmental, or acquired limitations in the exercise of a capacity do not by themselves remove personhood.


Question How should personhood be identified when direct evidence of the relevant capacities is unavailable.


Response

Personal Deism distinguishes personhood itself from the practical presumptions used to identify persons.


Question

Would an artificial intelligence be judged by a different standard of personhood than a biological being?


Response

No.


Personal Deism applies the same fundamental criteria of personhood regardless of biological origin.


Biological humanity is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself to establish the philosophical definition of personhood.


Likewise, artificial origin is neither sufficient to establish nor sufficient to exclude personhood.

The relevant question is whether the being genuinely possesses the characteristics constitutive of personhood.


Question

What does panentheistic mean?


Response

A panentheistic worldview holds that the divine pervades and interconnects the entire universe, while also existing independently of it. In this view, the universe is contained within the divine, but the divine is greater than the physical cosmos rather than identical to it.


Personal Deism does not regard its present formulation as infallible. Its foundational commitments are held as the deepest commitments of the system, while their interpretation and the conclusions derived from them remain subject to philosophical examination. A discovery or argument that genuinely undermines a non-foundational belief should result in its revision or rejection.


Principle

Personal Deism distinguishes what it assumes as foundational, what it argues from those assumptions, and what it recommends as practical policy. A conclusion must not be presented as empirically established merely because it is foundational, and a practical policy must not be treated as a metaphysical truth merely because the system recommends it.


Foundational commitments are not necessarily conclusions derived from other commitments within the system. A foundational commitment is a proposition that the philosophical system takes as a starting point for its internal development rather than deriving from other propositions within the system.

Foundational does not mean infallible, empirically demonstrated, or immune to philosophical criticism.


Personal Deism treats intrinsic dignity as a foundational moral reality, supported by the equal moral standing of persons, moral experience, rational reflection, and the conviction that persons participate in the divine.


Personal Deism has five foundational commitments from which its broader philosophical system is developed. These commitments are foundational in the sense that they serve as starting points for the system rather than conclusions derived from its other claims. They are nevertheless subject to philosophical examination, criticism, and possible revision. Their foundational status therefore does not make them infallible or immune from rational evaluation.

1. Persons have intrinsic dignity.
2. The divine exists and is intentional and good.
3. Persons participate in the divine.
4. Sentient creatures possess five fundamental freedoms.
5. Moral growth is the objective purpose of personal existence.

These commitments have different philosophical roles. The first and fourth are foundational normative commitments; the second and third are foundational metaphysical commitments; and the fifth is a foundational teleological commitment. The system's subsequent ethical, political, and social conclusions are developed from these commitments together with reasoned interpretation and empirical knowledge.


Question

Are all five foundational commitments equally independent of one another?


Response

No.


The five foundational commitments are foundational to the system as a whole, but they do not necessarily function in exactly the same way within the system.


Some foundational commitments establish the basic metaphysical and moral starting points of Personal Deism. Other commitments establish fundamental normative orientations from which further conclusions are developed.


The five foundational commitments are:

1. Persons have dignity.

2. The divine exists, and is intentional and good.

3. Persons participate in the divine.

4. All sentient creatures, even if not persons, have five freedoms.

5. Moral growth is the objective purpose of personal existence.


The philosophy may subsequently provide reasons for affirming relationships among these commitments. Such reasoning does not make the commitments non-foundational. It explains their coherence and implications within the system.


A foundational commitment is therefore a starting point of the philosophical system, not necessarily an isolated proposition that has no conceptual relationship to any other foundational commitment.


Personal Deism distinguishes between:

Foundational commitment — accepted as a starting point of the system.

Metaphysical grounding — an account of why a foundational commitment is intelligible or coherent within the system.

Reasoned consequence — a proposition derived from one or more foundational commitments.

Practical application — the manner in which those commitments and consequences are applied to particular circumstances.


A proposition may therefore be foundational while also receiving philosophical support from the broader metaphysical framework.


Moral standing may not be exhausted by ordinary human capacities or social membership. The same principle applies to any being that satisfies the philosophy's criteria for personhood, regardless of biological origin.


Personal Deism believes the divine is good because of a metaphysical argument concerning the nature of ultimate reality.
